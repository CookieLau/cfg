Networking is no longer optional but is now a core career skill. Studies from LinkedIn and MIT show that “weak ties” (people you don’t know well yet) often open the most doors to new opportunities. In Singapore’s fast-moving job market, where young professionals actively use LinkedIn and live events to get ahead, the ability to network confidently gives you a real edge.

In this highly interactive workshop, students will learn a simple PREP framework: Prepare, Research, Engage and Practice, to help build meaningful connections and speak to employers with clarity and confidence, especially in the midst of preparation for NUS Career Fest 2027.

Participants will learn to:

  • Enter any networking event with clear goals, a targeted list of employers/contacts, and a 30–60 second elevator pitch that sets you apart.
  • Introduce yourself with confidence using effective verbal and non-verbal communication tools to make a strong first impression.
  • Navigate real networking conversations smoothly, start conversations without awkwardness, ask thoughtful questions, sustain engagement, and follow up professionally on LinkedIn.

As businesses navigate climate risks and accelerate their sustainability efforts, finance is playing an increasingly important role in driving the transition towards a low-carbon economy.

From 2020 to 2024, global climate finance doubled from USD1.02 trillion to USD 2.01 trillion. Despite this significant growth, current climate financing remains well below the estimated global needs of USD 7.8 trillion per year to 2030, which will rise to USD 9 trillion per year for 2031 to 2035. (Data quoted from Climate Policy Initiative, Global Landscape of Climate Finance 2026)

But what does a career in sustainable finance actually look like, and how can you be part of this growing sector? Join industry professionals as they share firsthand insights into the world of sustainable finance, emerging industry trends and the diverse career pathways that combine financial expertise with sustainability impact.

Here's what you will discover:

  • Decode Sustainable Finance: Understand how finance supports climate action, manages environmental risks and drives the transition towards a more sustainable economy.
  • Explore Careers with Impact: Discover career pathways in ESG, asset management, investment analysis and other sustainability-related roles across industries.
  • Connect Finance with Sustainability: Gain insights into how professionals mobilise capital, develop sustainable financing solutions and help businesses translate sustainability goals into measurable action.
  • Get Industry-Ready: Hear from industry professionals about emerging opportunities, the skills employers value and how to position yourself for a career in sustainable finance.

Who should attend:

  • Soon-to-graduate students exploring career opportunities in Sustainable Finance such as ESG specialist, asset manager and investment analyst.
  • Students interested in Sustainability-related career pathways across different industries and sectors.

Singapore has firmly established itself as a regional leader in healthcare, with a strong and growing allied health sector that underpins the country's broader vision of accessible, high-quality care for its ageing population. Allied health professionals including physiotherapists, occupational therapists, speech therapists, dietitians, medical social workers, and diagnostic radiographers, among others, play an essential role alongside doctors and nurses in supporting patient recovery, rehabilitation, and long-term wellness.

With Singapore's population ageing rapidly and healthcare needs becoming more complex, the demand for allied health professionals is projected to rise significantly, and the sector has been identified as a key growth area under national healthcare manpower plans. Alongside this, there is a rising need for professionals who can deliver person-centred, multidisciplinary care from diagnosis through to rehabilitation and community reintegration.

Despite the sector's importance, many students may have limited awareness of the diverse career pathways within allied health and lack exposure to the day-to-day realities, skills, and training required to enter this field.
